How it works

  1. 1
    You give. Once, or a few dollars a month.
  2. 2
    It becomes permanent capital. Placed mostly in Ukrainian government bonds and other reliable instruments. The capital itself is never spent.
  3. 3
    Only the yield is paid out. Every month, to pensioners – starting at 1,000 UAH per person, designed to grow over time.

You don't get anything back – this is a donation, not a financial product. What lasts forever is the capital, not a guaranteed lifetime payment to any one person.

Each year the fund can take only a limited share of the capital. It's a safety limit, not a guaranteed income (Charter, cl. 5.7). Payouts grow from the 0.5% reserve and when money is freed up (cl. 7.5).

Where each dollar goes

We show this split before you give, as the Charter requires (cl. 4.6).

87¢
10¢
  • 87¢ – permanent capital. Never spent.
  • 10¢ – running the fund. Staff, accounting, audit, reports.
  • 3¢ – payment fees. Charged by banks and payment services.

Every year, the capital's yield pays for:

  • Monthly payouts to pensioners.
  • 1% of the capital a year for managing the capital and the program. It comes out of the yield, not the capital: on $100,000 that's $1,000 a year.
  • 0.5% of the capital kept back so payouts can grow.
  • A reserve for funeral assistance.

Example: $1,000,000 of capital, one year

Good year: earned $100,000Bad year: earned $20,000
Payouts and funeral reserve (3.5%)$35,000$35,000
Managing the capital and the program (1%)$10,000$10,000
Reserve for payout growth (0.5%)$5,000$5,000
Spent in total$50,000$50,000
Left in the fund / taken from the reserve+$50,000−$30,000

The fund spends the same planned share every year (5% of the capital in this model). In a good year the rest stays in the fund; in a bad year the gap is covered from the reserve (cl. 5.7–5.9). $35,000 a year is about 125 pensioners at 1,000 UAH a month. The exact share will be set by the investment policy.

Questions people ask

What if the hryvnia falls or inflation rises?

The calculator uses real yield, which is yield after inflation. Payouts are in hryvnias, so the fund can adjust them under published program rules. That's why you can test a pessimistic 3%.

What if Ukraine can't pay its bonds?

That risk is real. Government bonds are the main holding, not the only one. The investment policy limits risk and requires diversification (cl. 5.6). The fund doesn't promise any yield (cl. 4.7).

Who controls the money?

A licensed bank or asset manager, not the founder (cl. 5.5, 10.3). A supervisory board oversees the director. Any deal with related people must be approved and published (cl. 10).

Who gets the payouts?

Pensioners with long work records and very small pensions, chosen by published criteria that apply equally to everyone (cl. 3). They confirm their details every six months (cl. 6.4). If money is short, there's a waiting list (cl. 6.3).

What happens when a pensioner dies?

Payments stop. The freed money goes to other participants or raises their payouts (cl. 7.3–7.5). It doesn't pass to heirs (cl. 7.2). The fund may also help with the funeral (cl. 7.6).

How much goes to running the fund?

10% of each gift, plus 1% of the capital a year. We show this before you give (cl. 4.6). The director's salary is published in the reports (cl. 9.9).

Can my gift be used for something else?

No. Money given for pensioners is never used for other programs (cl. 2.5, 4.9).

Can I get my money back?

No. It's a donation: you don't get anything back, now or later (cl. 2.4).

Is it tax-deductible?

Not at this stage.

Why not just give to a pensioner directly?

Direct help runs out. Here the capital stays, and its yield keeps paying year after year.

Why do you need my email if you don't take money?

To count real interest, and to write to you once if the fund launches. Nothing else.
